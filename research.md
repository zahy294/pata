## At a glance

- **Organizer:** ETHGlobal, as part of Devcon 8 week; the hackathon shares the Devcon venue ([official page](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai), [FAQ](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)).
- **Format:** in person only, 36 hours. Remote participation isn't allowed; no-shows are marked withdrawn ([FAQ](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)).
- **Venue:** Jio World Convention Centre, G Block, BKC, Bandra East, Mumbai ([official page](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)).
- **Team:** 1–5 people. Every member applies and stakes separately. Solo is allowed, but they recommend at least two ([FAQ](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)).
- **Eligibility:** developers and builders, selected by application. Priority goes to people who can show they code, through GitHub and CV ([FAQ](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)).
- **Cost:** free, including food. You stake a small amount of crypto to confirm your spot, and get it back about 2–3 weeks after the event if you submit a project, even a partial one. The stake amount isn't published publicly ([FAQ](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)).
- **Travel and stay:** not covered. There are a few first-come nap rooms and no showers, so book a place to stay ([FAQ](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)).
- **Scale:** the page says 800+ attendees, 11+ protocols and 29+ workshops ([official page](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)).

## Submissions

From the [Mumbai hacker guide](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/info/details):

- **Form, on the Hacker Dashboard:** title, description and repo link. You can edit until the deadline.
- **Demo video:** optional but strongly encouraged. It must be **2–4 minutes**, at least 720p, and not sped up. No phone recording, no AI voiceover, no music-plus-captions instead of talking. The guide says to use slides to summarize key points, **at most 4 bullets per slide**.
- **Partner prizes:** pick up to **3 partners** in the last step. All tracks of one partner count as one pick. For each partner you explain how you used their tools and give feedback.
- **Two submission modes:** "Finalist and Partner Prizes", which requires the finalist judging session, or "Partner Prizes Only".
- **Template:** none. There is no slide deck round; the live demo and the video are the pitch.

## Judging

- **Finalist judging session:** **7 minutes per team**, a 4-minute demo then 3 minutes of Q&A. Common questions: what inspired you, which tools and why, what challenges you solved ([hacker guide](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/info/details)).
- **Five criteria:** Technicality, Originality, Practicality ("could it be used by its target audience today?"), Usability (UI/UX/DX) and WOW factor. No weights are published ([hacker guide](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/info/details)).
- **Finals:** 10 projects present on stage at the closing ([New Delhi recap](https://paragraph.com/@blog.ethglobal/ethglobal-new-delhi-2025-where-india-met-ethereum-s-future)).
- **Partner judging:** done from your submission materials. Booth visits are optional ([hacker guide](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/info/details)).
- **Finalist prize:** still not listed for Mumbai on Nov 1. At New York 2026 each finalist member got 1,000 USDC, $500 flight reimbursement for another 2026 hackathon, a hoodie, and the team got $10k in AWS credits ([NY 2026 prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/newyork2026/prizes)). New Delhi 2025 was the same ([New Delhi prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/newdelhi/prizes)).

## Tracks and problem statements

The event is open-ended: build anything on Ethereum ([FAQ](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)). Listed themes: ZK proofs, AI × crypto, DeFi, crypto consumer, L2s, interoperability, public goods, privacy and security, TEEs, data availability, identity, dev tools ([official page](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)).

**New for 2026: three ways to start** ([official page](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai), [rules](https://ethglobal.com/rules)):

1. **From Scratch (Classic):** empty repo at kick-off.
2. **Extend Open Source (Continuity):** bring a repo you maintain and ship a new feature.
3. **Ship a Feature (Continuity):** build a new open-source feature on top of an existing private product.

Continuity entries must document what existed before, in writing to ETHGlobal and in the repo, video and description, and must add substantive new work. Undisclosed prior work can mean disqualification and a ban ([rules](https://ethglobal.com/rules)). At the track's debut in New York (June 2026), 61 of 232 projects (26%) used Continuity, 3 of the 10 finalists came through it, and 23 Continuity projects won partner prizes ([ETHGlobal blog](https://paragraph.com/@blog.ethglobal/inside-the-continuity-track-debut)).

Prize-specific "extra points" worth knowing:

- **Hedera x402:** spending controls for agents (budgets, per-merchant limits, allowlists, human approval above a set amount), ERC-8004 or HCS-14 agent identity, and an HCS topic as a tamper-evident log of each agent's spending. Needs an agent that completes a paid request end to end with no human approving, the Hedera transaction ID in the README, and a demo video of 5 minutes or less ([prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)).
- **Arc:** a working frontend and backend, an architecture diagram, the Arc Studio project URL, and Studio shown in the demo ([prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)).
- **EF CROPS:** a README section with one or two lines per letter. Since Nov 1 the brief defines what each letter must say:
  - **C:** what you control that the user depends on (domain, server, RPC, relayer), and what happens if it goes away or refuses them.
  - **O:** the licence, and whether a user can build and run it from the repo alone.
  - **P:** what each third party (your server, RPC provider, analytics, indexers) learns about the user.
  - **S:** how keys and data are protected, and the trust assumptions.

  The demo (3 minutes at most) must show the moment the property matters, for example switching off your backend, swapping the RPC provider, or showing server logs with nothing identifying in them. No third-party trackers. Judges rank a structural property ("works without you") above a cosmetic one (a setting), and ask whether someone would use it without knowing what CROPS means. The brief is still marked "tbc: final review pending" ([prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)).
- **Arkiv:** each track is scored 25% on each of four criteria, and each criterion gets its own file in the repo. For Build on Arkiv:
  - feedback: `/arkiv/friction.md`;
  - reach: `/arkiv/reach.md`;
  - why Arkiv: `/arkiv/why-arkiv.md`. "If you swapped Arkiv for Postgres, what would your users lose? What did you keep off Arkiv, and why?";
  - how you use Arkiv: `/arkiv/how-you-use-arkiv.md`. Do the filters, editing rights and expiry fit the app, and does each feature change something users see?

  Also required: `/arkiv/schema.md`, plus the writing wallet addresses and one query judges can run, both in the README. Not counted: the SDK installed but never used to read, or Arkiv used only as a log nothing reads ([prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)).
- **0G:** every track asks for proof of real 0G use (a Compute request ID, a Storage root hash, a Chain contract address). "Not wanted": generic chat wrappers, and 0G used only for logs. The Wildcard also asks for one sentence on what breaks if 0G is removed ([prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)).
- **ENS:** ENSv2 features must be central; expiring, revocable or non-transferable subnames and agents-as-namespaces are named as what they want to see ([prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)).

## Rules and gotchas

- **Prior work:**
  - Classic track: anything built before kick-off makes you ineligible for partner prizes and finalist. Public libraries and starter kits are fine; disclose them ([hacker guide](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/info/details)).
  - Continuity track: disclose everything that existed before; new parts must be open source ([rules](https://ethglobal.com/rules)).
- **Version control throughout:** repos with "single commits of large files without proper history" are assumed unqualified ([rules](https://ethglobal.com/rules)). 1inch also requires "no single-commit entries on the final day" ([prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)).
- **AI tools are allowed, with conditions** ([hacker guide](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/info/details)):
  - Document which files and parts were AI-assisted.
  - Projects built "entirely" by AI may lose partner and finalist eligibility.
  - With spec-driven workflows (Kiro, spec-kit and similar), the specs, prompts and planning files must be in the repo. This applies to Claude Code plans too.
- **Limits:** 3 partner prizes and up to 3 sponsor SDKs per project; one team per person ([official page](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai), [FAQ](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)).
- **After submission:** stop working until prizes are paid out ([FAQ](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)).
- **Stake:** forfeited if you don't submit a project ([FAQ](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)).
- **Waitlist:** a waitlisted person can't join an accepted team until they're accepted themselves ([FAQ](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)).

## Clashes and risks

- **Mid-sem exams, Nov 2–4:** they end the day before the hackathon starts on Thu Nov 5. There's no overlap, but you'd travel Bengaluru to Mumbai on Nov 4 evening or Nov 5 and arrive tired. Prep (team, idea, tooling) was meant to finish before Nov 2; the idea and research were finalized on Nov 3, and accounts and wallet funding are still open (see idea.md's status). You'll miss Devcon 8 (Nov 3–6) except possibly its last day.
- **Arrival on Thu Nov 5:** registration closes at 10 PM, so arriving later risks being marked withdrawn. To catch the ENSv2 workshop (4:00 PM) and the EF workshop (5:00 PM), be at Pavilion 2 by about 3:30 PM. The Arkiv workshop is also at 4:00 PM, in the other room: split the team, or catch Arkiv at its booth.
- **Accepted (Oct 21):** if you haven't yet, stake to confirm your spot; the stake is forfeited only if you don't submit a project ([FAQ](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)). Each teammate needs their own acceptance and stake.
- **Selection odds:** competitive. New Delhi drew 1,600 hackers; Mumbai's capacity is listed as 800+ attendees. A thin GitHub profile is the most common rejection reason.
- **Costs:** BKC hotels in Devcon week will be expensive, and no stay is provided. Two nights near BKC, or sharing with teammates, is worth booking now.
- **Prize details:** all twelve partners had published their tracks by Nov 1. EF's CROPS brief is still marked "tbc: final review pending", so recheck the [prizes page](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes) on Nov 5.
- **World ID is hard to get in India.** World stopped Orb verification in India in 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/12/20/worldcoin-is-no-longer-offering-orb-verification-in-india-brazil-and-france/)). On Oct 21 its [Orb finder](https://world.org/find-orb) listed only the UK, Germany, Japan, the US, South Korea and Singapore, and the passport credential's country list doesn't include India ([World blog](https://world.org/blog/announcements/new-world-id-passport-credential-launches-access-wld-tokens)). World's lighter "Selfie Check" exists, but no country list was found. Any idea that needs Indian hackers to hold a verified World ID is at risk; ask World on Discord before building on it. **Update, Nov 1:** World's Mumbai Agent Pass track now mocks proofs with fake identities, so a hackathon demo no longer needs an Orb. Real users in India still can't verify, which hurts any pitch about Indian users.
- **Indian alternatives for proof of personhood (not partners):** [Self](https://docs.celo.org/build-on-celo/build-with-self) proves a passport, EU ID or Aadhaar on the phone with zero knowledge, and is actively maintained. [Anon Aadhaar](https://github.com/anon-aadhaar/anon-aadhaar), from the EF's PSE team, proves Aadhaar in the browser, but its repo was last pushed in April 2025.
